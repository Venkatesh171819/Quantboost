01 — The Idea

what problem is this solving

Most trading models look brilliant right up until real money touches them. This project asks a harder question than "can I predict stock returns?" It asks: if I built a model that looked good, how would I know whether that's real, or whether I just tricked myself? Almost every part of this system exists to answer that second question, not the first.

Think of it like this

It's the difference between a student who's confident because they memorized last year's exam, and one who's confident because they were tested on questions they'd never seen, under exam conditions, more than once. This project is built almost entirely around the second kind of confidence.

02 — The System

nine modules, each one checking the last

MOD.01DATA

Real prices — or a deliberate fake

Pulls 14 years of daily prices for 24 assets (stocks, bonds, gold, sector ETFs). It can also generate entirely fake, simulated market data on purpose — if the model claims to find a pattern in data that's mathematically pure noise, that proves something in the pipeline is broken.

data.py · simulate_panel()
MOD.02FEATURES

A much deeper clue sheet

Momentum, volatility, and reversal, plus how easily a stock could actually be traded, how much "memory" its price carries, and how random or trending it currently looks. Every clue is measured relative to the other assets on that same day, not in isolation.

features.py · 30+ signals
MOD.03LABELING

Graded like a real trade

Instead of just asking "did the price go up," each day is judged on which happens first: hitting a profit target, hitting a stop-loss, or running out of time — the way an actual trade would be judged. Overlapping, repetitive stretches are down-weighted so one event isn't counted five times.

labeling.py · triple_barrier()
MOD.04VALIDATION

Deliberately paranoid testing

Training data that sits even slightly too close in time to the test period gets deleted, with a safety buffer on top. The strategy is also tested across many different combinations of time blocks, so the result is a range, not one lucky number.

cv.py · PurgedKFold, walk_forward_splits()
MOD.05MODELS

Three engines, one deliberate underdog

XGBoost and CatBoost — two modern, powerful methods — compete against AdaBoost, an older, weaker method kept in on purpose as a sanity-check loser. Settings are auto-tuned across dozens of trials, optimizing for how well the model ranks assets, not raw accuracy.

models.py, tuning.py · Optuna, 25+ trials
MOD.06PORTFOLIO

Rankings become real positions

Turns "which assets look best" into an actual long/short portfolio — balanced, risk-capped per position, and automatically scaled up or down so the portfolio doesn't get riskier just because the market got wilder.

portfolio.py · vol targeting, Ledoit-Wolf
MOD.07COSTS

Stress-tested against trading friction

The whole backtest is re-run at 2x, 3x, and 5x the assumed cost of trading. A strategy that only survives at unrealistically cheap costs isn't a strategy — it's a mirage.

costs.py, backtest.py · cost_sensitivity()
MOD.08RISK

How bad could a bad day get — really?

Estimates the worst realistic daily loss, then checks whether that estimate is honest — not just how often losses exceeded it, but whether those breaches happened in suspicious clusters, a sign the risk model missed a regime shift.

risk.py · VaR, Expected Shortfall, Kupiec/Christoffersen
MOD.09HONESTY

"Did I just get lucky?" — with math

Calculates the odds that an impressive-looking result is real, after accounting for how many different versions of the model were tried. Trying 25 configurations and picking the best one gets penalized here — on purpose.

tuning.py · Deflated Sharpe, Probability of Overfitting

In plain terms: one test literally cuts off the last two months of a stock's price history and recomputes every feature — if any value changes, that feature was secretly using information it shouldn't have had. Another feeds the model pure random numbers and confirms it correctly finds nothing. This is what "leakage-safe" means when you actually go and check it, rather than just claiming it.

04 — The Discipline

why the results aren't just a lucky backtest

✓

Tested against pure noise

A version of the pipeline is fed data with a known, controlled answer — including data that's pure randomness — to confirm it correctly reports "no signal" when there truly is none.

✓

Penalized for trying too hard

The headline "skill" score is deflated based on how many different model configurations were tested before landing on the best one — because trying enough things eventually looks good by chance alone.

✓

Stress-tested, not just tested

Trading costs are multiplied up to 5x their realistic level, and the risk model's own predictions are checked for honesty — not just graded once and trusted.

✓

Graded like a real trade

Outcomes are labeled by which barrier — profit, loss, or timeout — was hit first, and repeated or overlapping periods are down-weighted so the model can't over-learn from one dramatic stretch of market history.

05 — The Fine Print

what this is — and isn't

⚠ Known limitations (from the project's own documentation)
  • Uses daily prices only — no intraday data, no real order-book fills.
  • The stock list is fixed as of today, so it doesn't fully correct for the fact that some historical "losers" have since been delisted or removed from indexes.
  • The cost of borrowing shares to short is a flat estimate, not tailored per stock.
  • By default, it validates against simulated market data, not exclusively live history — a deliberate choice for testing honesty, but worth knowing if asked directly.
